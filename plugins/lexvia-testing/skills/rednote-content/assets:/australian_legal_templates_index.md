# Australian Legal Content Templates

These three Chinese-language templates preserve the checklist, story and myth-busting approaches. They are topic-neutral and intended for Chinese-speaking Xiaohongshu readers seeking information about Australian law.

No legal topic is preselected. Choose the subject for each post and verify the applicable Australian federal, state or territory jurisdiction before filling in the placeholders.

| Template filename | Focus | Legal topic | Jurisdiction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [australia_legal_checklist.md](australia_legal_checklist.md) | Practical checklists that help readers in Australia identify relevant facts, gather information and understand next steps; designed for saving and later reference. | Any selected Australian legal issue suited to a checklist; no fixed topic. | Relevant Australian federal, state or territory jurisdiction, to be specified for each post. |
| [australia_legal_story.md](australia_legal_story.md) | Relatable Australian scenarios or verified cases that explain how facts and evidence affect legal reasoning; designed for reading engagement and trust. | Any selected Australian legal issue suited to a case or scenario narrative; no fixed topic. | Relevant Australian federal, state or territory jurisdiction, to be specified for each post. |
| [australia_legal_myth_buster.md](australia_legal_myth_buster.md) | Clear answers to legal questions and distinctions between easily confused concepts in Australia; designed for understanding, sharing and informed discussion. | Any selected Australian legal question or misconception suited to clarification; no fixed topic. | Relevant Australian federal, state or territory jurisdiction, to be specified for each post. |

## Preserved writing structures

- Checklist: specific problem → practical value → structured checklist → next steps → applicable conditions.
- Story: personal situation → central dispute → key facts and evidence → verified outcome or possible pathways → practical lessons.
- Myth-busting: question → direct answer → concept distinctions → applicable conditions → practical action.

## Usage notes

- Template copy and placeholders are in Chinese. Filenames, focus descriptions and legal topic fields are in English.
- Choose a topic at the time of use; none of the templates is restricted to a particular area of Australian law.
- Label hypothetical stories clearly. Use verified sources for real cases and outcomes.
- Any legal terminology must not be translated from English to Chinese.
