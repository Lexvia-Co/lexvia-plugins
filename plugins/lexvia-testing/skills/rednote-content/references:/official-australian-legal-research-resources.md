# Legal Research Procedure with Official Australian Legal Research Resources

## Overview

A legal research procedure for answering general legal questions with a broad categorized source directory of official websites and other publicly available online resources that a legal practitioner can consult. 

## Required Information before use
- A legal topic for conducting research

## Scope and inclusion criteria

- **Jurisdiction:** NSW plus relevant Commonwealth law, continuing the working assumption inferred from the PDF’s NCAT and conveyancing references. This is a topic-focused directory, not a survey of every state and territory.
- **Official sources:** government departments, parliaments, courts, tribunals, statutory bodies and their publications. Two specialist sources are explicitly identified: [NAATI is jointly government-owned](https://www.naati.com.au/about-us/), and [NSW Land Registry Services is the authorised private operator of the NSW land-title registry](https://www.registrargeneral.nsw.gov.au/regulator). Their authority is limited to their respective functions.
- **Public access:** listed information is public. Some associated transactions, searches, certificates, registrations or services attract fees or require an account; those paid outputs are not assumed to be freely available.
- **Categories:** labels follow the PDF. “BFA” means Binding Financial Agreement. Cross-category labels identify other relevant topic areas, not exhaustive subject coverage of the whole website.
- **Source types:** legislation and judgments provide primary authority; official guides explain practice; registers and maps supply factual research inputs. Official publication does not make every guide legally binding or current.

There are **55 resource entries**, comprising websites, topic portals and selected official publications. Several entries belong to the same publisher but serve distinct research purposes. Each entry appears under its main topic; cross-category labels identify reuse. Use the shared primary-source section alongside each topic section.

## Legal Research Workflow 
**The scope of the following research process must fall within the scope and included online resources defined before**

1. Start with the relevant topic’s official guidance to identify the legal issue and terminology.
2. Verify material propositions against the applicable legislation, instruments and judgments in the shared sources. 
3. Check jurisdiction, commencement, historical application, later case treatment and the publication date of guidance before drafting.
4. Record the exact source URL, provision or judgment paragraph, and date checked in the eventual answer’s research notes.
5. Write up a legal research report on chosen topic following the template `assets/legal-research-report-template.md`

## Gotchas
- Read a PDF publication’s date and check its publisher for a replacement edition. Publicly released migration policy may be historical or incomplete. Registry information, planning maps and general guides do not replace a particular contract, title search, trust deed or other matter-specific evidence.
