# Terminology preservation for Chinese Rednote content

Read this reference after the skill's excluded-topic check and before translating research, drafting Chinese copy, or editing an existing draft. Apply it again after tone editing and during final Word review. The topic restriction takes precedence: do not research excluded terminology or create a glossary as a workaround for an excluded request.

## What stays in English

- Preserve specialist terms used in their legal, migration, tax, finance, property or business sense, including named rights, roles, arrangements, instruments and procedures.
- Preserve official names of institutions, legislation, cases, forms, programs, registers and services, together with their verified English acronyms and identifiers. Keep Act years, form numbers and case citations intact when present.
- Translate ordinary narrative into Simplified Chinese. A word's role in context determines whether it is a specialist term: do not leave every ordinary noun in English merely because it appears on a legal or government website. For example, `consideration` as a contract-law concept is protected; a conversational use of the same word is ordinary prose.
- The starter list below is not exhaustive. An unlisted specialist term or official name still stays in English after verification; absence from the table is not permission to translate it.

## How to use terms in Chinese prose

1. Use the exact English term appropriate to the topic and jurisdiction. Preserve meaningful qualifiers and the source's official spelling; do not substitute a similar concept from another jurisdiction.
2. When an acronym is useful, introduce the verified English full name followed by the acronym in parentheses, then use it consistently. English-only expansions such as `Binding Financial Agreement (BFA)` are allowed. Do not invent acronyms, guess an expansion, or add a Chinese name in parentheses. A missing acronym in the table means none is prescribed here.
3. Chinese sentences may explain what the term does, whom it concerns, or its practical effect, based on verified research. Do not present a Chinese equivalent name, a dictionary-style definition pairing, a transliteration, or a Chinese replacement label used later in the post. An official Chinese translation does not override this editorial rule.
4. When rewriting a Chinese draft, identify already-translated specialist terms and restore the verified English wording. Confirm the intended concept from context and sources; do not blindly reverse-translate an ambiguous Chinese label.
5. Apply the same policy to titles, headings, examples, captions, calls to action and hashtags. For a hashtag that cannot accommodate a multiword term, use a verified acronym, choose a general audience/location tag, or omit the tag; do not invent a Chinese equivalent or a compressed English name.
6. Keep source links, term verification notes and uncertainty notes in the English research report or other research notes under the skill's intermediate-output location. Do not insert them into publishable copy contrary to the template rules. Ordinary content generation must not edit this reference or the installed plugin.

### Verification and uncertainty

- For each topic, record the chosen English term, any acronym expansion, jurisdiction, exact source URL and date checked in research notes. Note the relevant provision, paragraph or form when needed. A directory link alone is not evidence for a specific meaning.
- Prefer the applicable primary authority for a legal definition and the issuing institution for its current official name. An official guide helps explain usage but does not override legislation or establish that a rule applies to every jurisdiction.
- If a term is absent here, use the appropriate source above or the research directory. If the wording is still uncertain, preserve a supplied English term provisionally in research notes, flag the gap there and verify before final delivery; do not invent an English term or a Chinese substitute. Ask for clarification when the intended concept cannot be resolved from the supplied material.
- Add entries to this reference only during an explicit skill-maintenance task, using the same term/acronym/subject/source structure. Do not turn one post's terminology notes into automatic plugin edits.

## Review before delivery

- [ ] Specialist terms and official names remain English throughout the draft, including any supplied text rewritten from Chinese. Context, jurisdiction and meaningful qualifiers are preserved.
- [ ] Acronyms have verified English expansions and are used consistently; no Chinese equivalent names, transliterations or bracketed translations have been introduced.
- [ ] Chinese explanations describe function or effect without introducing replacement terminology. Ordinary narrative remains natural Simplified Chinese.
- [ ] Titles, headings, captions, calls to action and hashtags follow the same policy; unavailable hashtag forms are omitted or replaced with general tags or verified acronyms.
- [ ] Unlisted terms and uncertainties have been checked against authoritative sources, with evidence recorded in research notes rather than publishable copy.
- [ ] Tone editing has not translated or renamed protected terms. The final Word document's text and relevant metadata retain the reviewed terminology, and the skill's existing topic and length checks pass.
